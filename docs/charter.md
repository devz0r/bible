# Translation Charter

**Working title:** *The Transparent Bible* (placeholder — check for name collisions before any public release)
**Status:** Draft 1, adopted 2026-11-03
**Governs:** every file in this repository. Where this charter and the [style guide](style-guide.md) or [glossary](glossary.md) disagree, this charter wins.

---

## 1. Purpose

To produce an English Bible that is:

1. **Accurate** — faithful to the words and meaning of the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek texts.
2. **Readable** — plain modern English that an ordinary reader can follow without a dictionary of archaisms.
3. **Transparent** — nothing hidden. Every place where the translator had to choose (between meanings, between manuscripts, between literal and natural English) is marked or footnoted, so the reader can see what was chosen and why.

We do not claim to end the debates between translations. Most of them are not about accuracy but about which manuscripts to follow, how to trade form against readability, and which theological traditions to honor. Our aim is to make every one of those choices **visible**, so any reader — including a committed King James reader — can check our work against the originals.

## 2. Foundational decisions

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **New Testament base text** | **Textus Receptus**, in Scrivener's 1894 edition (the Greek text underlying the King James Version). Readings from the **critical text** (NA28 / SBLGNT) and the **Majority/Byzantine text** (Robinson-Pierpont 2018) are given in footnotes wherever they differ in meaning. | Keeps the textual base KJV readers trust, while hiding nothing from readers who prefer the older manuscripts. This is the approach of the NKJV, but with fuller and more candid notes. |
| 1b | **Old Testament base text** | **Masoretic Text** (Westminster Leningrad Codex). Significant readings from the Dead Sea Scrolls, Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch and other ancient versions are footnoted. | The Masoretic Text is the base of the KJV and of virtually every modern Protestant translation. The KJV's own Hebrew text (Bomberg's Second Rabbinic Bible, 1524–25) differs from Leningrad only in minor details; where a difference matters, it is noted. |
| 2 | **The divine name (YHWH)** | **LORD** (small capitals). *Adonai YHWH* = "Lord GOD". *YHWH Elohim* = "LORD God". *YHWH Tseva'ot* = "LORD of hosts". The meaning and pronunciation of the name are explained in notes at Exodus 3:14–15 and 6:3. | Follows the KJV, the Jewish reading tradition, and the New Testament's own practice (it renders the name as *Kyrios*, "Lord"). The small capitals tell the reader exactly where the Hebrew has the name. |
| 3 | **Canon** | The **66 books** of the Protestant canon, in the KJV order. The Apocrypha may be added later as a clearly separate section (as in the 1611 KJV), after the 66 books are complete. | Finish the core first. The Apocrypha is optional and does not delay anything else. |
| 4 | **Gender language** | **No gender-inclusive language.** Masculine forms are translated as masculine: *adelphoi* = "brothers", *anthrōpos* = "man", *huioi* = "sons", generic "he" stays "he". Where the original itself names both sexes ("sons and daughters", "men and women"), so does the translation. | Decided by the project owner. It is also the more formally literal choice. |
| 5 | **License** | The translation text is dedicated to the **public domain (CC0 1.0)**. Anyone may copy, quote, print, sell or adapt it without permission. We *ask* (but cannot require) that modified versions use a different name. | Maximum freedom to read, share and build on Scripture. The KJV itself is effectively public domain almost everywhere, and the BSB and WEB took this route. |
| 6 | **Versification** | **English (KJV) verse numbering.** Where the Hebrew numbering differs (Psalm titles, Joel, Malachi 4, and others), the Hebrew reference is given in a note at the first affected verse. | Readers, commentaries and concordances in English use KJV numbering. |
| 7 | **Register** | Modern English. No *thee/thou/-eth*, including in prayers. | Readability is a core goal. The useful precision of *thee* (singular) versus *ye* (plural) is kept with a separate marker (see §4). |

## 3. Translation principles, in priority order

When principles conflict, the higher one wins **and the conflict is footnoted**.

1. **Accuracy of meaning.** Say what the original says — no more, no less.
2. **Do not resolve what the original leaves open.** If the Hebrew or Greek can mean two things and English can keep both, keep both (for example, "the faith of Jesus Christ" in Galatians 2:16, which the Greek leaves open between "faith *in*" and "faithfulness *of*"). If English must choose, choose, and note the alternative.
3. **Preserve form where English allows.** Keep the original's imagery, repetitions, wordplay, idioms and key terms, unless doing so would mislead a modern reader. Word order is not preserved; English grammar governs.
4. **Concordance.** The same source word is rendered by the same English word wherever context allows. The [glossary](glossary.md) sets the default for key terms. Departures are allowed when context demands them, and are noted at least the first time in each book.
5. **Readability.** Plain, natural, dignified English. Established theological vocabulary is kept (righteousness, justify, sanctify, propitiation, atonement, covenant, grace, redemption) because these are technical terms, and replacing them with paraphrases loses precision. Each is explained in the glossary.
6. **Transparency.** Anything the reader would need to know to check our choice goes in a footnote.

### What this translation does not do

- **No paraphrase.** We do not convert ancient money, weights, measures, times or customs into modern ones in the text. A *denarius* stays a denarius, and a footnote gives the equivalent.
- **No silent additions.** Every English word with no counterpart in the source (beyond what grammar requires) is marked as *supplied*, the way the KJV uses italics.
- **No silent omissions.** Every source word or particle is accounted for, including *hinneh* / *idou* ("behold") and the conjunctions.
- **No capitalized pronouns for God** (he, him, his). The original languages make no such distinction, and capitalizing forces interpretive decisions, especially in messianic psalms. This follows the KJV.
- **No updating of theology.** The translation does not soften, modernize or explain away anything the text says.

## 4. Transparency devices

These are the project's distinctive features. They are defined fully in the [style guide](style-guide.md).

| Device | What it shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Supplied words** (italics) | English words added for sense, with no corresponding word in the source. | John 8:24: "unless you believe that I am *he*". The Greek says only "I am", so "he" is set in italics, as in the KJV. |
| **Plural "you" marker** | Which "you"s are plural in the original. This recovers what the KJV's *ye/you* versus *thee/thou* showed. | Luke 22:31–32: Satan demanded to have *you* (plural: all the disciples) … but I have prayed for *you* (singular: Peter). |
| **LORD / GOD** in small capitals | Where the Hebrew has the divine name YHWH. | "The LORD is my shepherd." |
| **Textual notes** | Where manuscripts differ, which reading is in the text and which manuscript traditions support each reading. Stated candidly, including when the Textus Receptus reading has weak manuscript support. | 1 John 5:7–8; Mark 16:9–20; John 7:53–8:11; Acts 8:37. |
| **Meaning notes** | Where a word has more than one meaning in play, or the English had to choose. | John 3:8 — *pneuma* means both "wind" and "Spirit"; Jesus is using both. |
| **Literal notes** | The literal wording when the text uses a more natural English rendering of an idiom. | "Get ready for action" — Hebrew/Greek: "gird up your loins". |
| **Words of Jesus** | Marked in the data so editions can print them in red if they choose. | — |

### Honesty about the Textus Receptus

Choosing the Textus Receptus as the base does not mean pretending every one of its readings is equally well attested. Where a Textus Receptus reading is found in few or no Greek manuscripts before the late medieval period (for example, 1 John 5:7b–8a, the "Johannine Comma"), it stays in the text, because that is our base, but the footnote says plainly what the manuscript evidence is. Readers on both sides of the debate should be able to trust our notes.

### Old Testament departures from the Masoretic Text

The Masoretic Text is followed unless another reading has **ancient manuscript support** and is clearly superior. Every such departure is footnoted. Example: Psalm 22:16, where the Masoretic Text reads "like a lion my hands and feet", while a Dead Sea Scrolls manuscript and the Septuagint support "they pierced my hands and feet" (the KJV reading).

### New Testament quotations of the Old Testament

Where the New Testament quotes the Old, the Old Testament passage is translated from the Hebrew on its own terms. Where the Hebrew allows a rendering consistent with the New Testament's use of it, that rendering is preferred, and the note explains the range of the Hebrew. Example: Isaiah 7:14, *'almah* = "virgin" (as quoted in Matthew 1:23 with Greek *parthenos*), with a note that the Hebrew word means a young woman of marriageable age.

This principle reflects the Christian canonical reading of Scripture. It is stated openly here so that readers know it is a principle and not an accident.

## 6. Workflow (per chapter)

1. **Prepare.** Load the source text, morphology and variant data for the chapter.
2. **Draft.** Translate according to this charter, the style guide and the glossary.
3. **Independent review.** A separate pass, ideally by a different model than the one that drafted, checks every verse against the source. It looks for omissions, additions, wrong senses, glossary departures and unmarked supplied words.
4. **Notes pass.** Confirm every required textual, meaning and literal note is present and accurate.
5. **Consistency check.** Automated scripts check glossary terms, markup validity and verse counts against the KJV versification.
6. **Log.** Record the chapter's status and any new decisions in the progress tracker. Any rule change goes into §8 below and is applied retroactively to already-translated chapters.

**Human review.** A pilot set of passages (Genesis 1–3, Psalm 23, Isaiah 7:14 and 53, John 1 and 3, Romans 3, 1 John 5) will be checked by human reviewers with training in Hebrew and Greek before full-scale translation begins. The finished translation will be labeled as **AI-assisted**.

## 7. Output format

The master text is **USFM 3** (Unified Standard Format Markers), one file per book. USFM is the standard format used by Bible societies and Bible software. Readable editions (web, EPUB, PDF, plain text) are generated from the USFM, so there is a single source of truth.
